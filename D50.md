# fm1-d50: a Roland D-50 on the M-VAVE FM-1

Status: a plan. No D-50 code exists yet. This repository starts as a copy of
[fm1-sf2](https://github.com/mwedmark/fm1-sf2) (remote `upstream`, its `main` at `3bf0074`),
because the device side is solved there: the toolchain, the audio hook, the second core, the flash upload, safe mode, the emulator workflow and
the install page. The goal is different: **a faithful D-50 clone that only runs the D-50, at 32 kHz, as close to the original as can be done.**
It is not a GM player, and it has no SoundFont.

Facts about the D-50 below that are marked *(verify)* come from memory and must be checked against the service manual, the owner's manual
and the MIDI implementation before code depends on them. Milestone 1's findings, with sources, are in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md).

## 1. What the D-50 is (the target)

- **Patch** = Upper tone + Lower tone, a key mode (Whole, Dual, Split, and the Separate / -S variants *(verify the full list)*), split point,
  balance, tone transposes, the patch's reverb type and level, portamento, and the bender and aftertouch assignment.
- **Tone** = Tone Common + **2 partials**. The tone's *structure* (1..7) says how the two partials combine: alone, or through the ring modulator.
  Tone Common also holds the pitch envelope, three LFOs, the EQ (low and high) and the chorus *(verify: 8 chorus types)*.
- **Partial**: the wave generator (a synth wave, square or saw with pulse width and PWM, or a PCM wave), pitch (coarse, fine, key follow,
  the LFO and envelope modes), **TVF** (cutoff, resonance, key follow, bias, its envelope, LFO depth: synth partials only), **TVA** (level,
  velocity, bias, its envelope, LFO depth). The envelopes have times and levels, a sustain point and an end point, and velocity and key-follow
  time scaling.
- **PCM waves**: 100, attack transients and loops, from the 512 KB wave ROM. Their start and length are in tables in the program EPROM.
- **Polyphony**: 32 partials, i.e. 16 voices for a Whole patch, 8 + 8 for Dual *(verify the allocation rules: Roland's voice stealing is part of the sound)*.
- **Rate**: the LA chip (MB87136A, the MT-32's part number) runs from 32.768 MHz, which gives **32 kHz** as in the MT-32 (not measured on a D-50).
  Then come the chorus ASIC, the reverb chip (its 32 types are in the silicon), the D/A and the analog output stage.
- **Control**: a NEC uPD78312 at 12 MHz (8 KB internal ROM, IC25, plus the 64 KB EPROM, IC22) runs Roland's firmware. It reads MIDI and the panel, computes the envelopes and LFOs, and writes the LA chip's
  registers. Most of what makes a D-50 sound like a D-50 (the envelope curves, the key-follow tables, the LFO shapes, the voice allocation) lives in that
  firmware, not in the LA chip.
- **MIDI**: Roland SysEx DT1/RQ1 with the D-50's address map. Patches, tones and partials are bytes at fixed addresses. The patch banks people have
  (`.syx`) are dumps of that memory, and they are the test material.

## 2. How faithful: two ways

| | A: run Roland's firmware | B: reimplement it (as Munt does for the MT-32) |
|---|---|---|
| What runs | A uPD78312 emulator running the D-50's control ROMs, plus an LA chip emulated at the register level, plus the reverb | Native C for the control logic (envelopes, LFOs, tables, allocation), plus the LA partial generator |
| Faithful in | Everything the firmware does, for free, including the bugs | What was reverse engineered and measured |
| Needs from the user | The control ROM, the wave ROM, the reverb ROM | The wave ROM only (synth partials need nothing) |
| Unknown | How the D-50 talks to its LA chip, register by register; how much of it MAME has; the cost on the pi32v2 | Every curve and table, one by one, against recordings |
| Panel | The D-50's own LCD and buttons have to be mapped to the FM-1's | Our own UI on the FM-1's screen |

**Decided in milestone 1 (RESEARCH.md section 4): B on the device, A on the PC as the answer key.** The device runs our own engine with the
firmware's tables read from the user's EPROM. A PC tool runs MAME's uPD78312 core on the user's ROMs with Nuked-MT32's decap-based LA32, and renders what
the real LA chip would give, so every curve on the device can be tested against it without a real D-50. The PC tool is never linked with the FM-1's
SDK, so its GPL parts are no problem there.

**Munt** (mt32emu, LGPL-2.1+, which can be combined with this repository's GPLv3) emulates the MT-32, a cut-down D-50 with the same LA chip family.
Its LA32 wave generator (synth waves, PCM playback, ring modulation, the log/exp tables) is the best known public reverse engineering of the chip,
and its TVA/TVF/TVP code shows how the MT-32 firmware drives it. The D-50's firmware is not the MT-32's, so the envelopes, the structures and the
parameter map have to be done for the D-50.

## 3. What comes from fm1-sf2 and what goes

Kept (device side, done and tested):
- `fm1_open/` with the SDK, `sdk/Build.ps1`, the JieLi toolchain (`D:\JL\pi32`), `build_fw.ps1`.
- The audio hook in `fm1_open/app/board/audio.c` (`fm1_audio_irq`, 128 stereo Q23 frames a block, `FM1B_SKIP_ENGINE` so the stock FM engine does not run).
- The 32 kHz render path, and the output stage (`sf2out.c`) with its 32 -> 44.1 kHz resampler. *Open: can the codec run at 32 kHz itself, so that
  nothing is resampled?*
- The second core (`cpu1_split.c`): partials are independent, so half of them can render on CPU1.
- The SysEx flash upload (`sf2_upload.c`, protocol `F0 7D 'S' 'F' ...`): it moves the wave ROM, and later patch banks, into the user flash.
- Safe mode, `install.html`, `start_emu.ps1` and fm1-emu, the WebAssembly test harness.

Goes (SF2-specific): the converter (`index.html`, `pack_bank.js`, `convert.py`), the FM1B bank format and `fm1b*.c`, the live cache and MP3,
SEA5/SEA6/BFP8, the MIDI file player and `song.html`, `soundfonts/`, the GM banks and `banks/`, the SF2 tests. They are removed in one commit
once the D-50 engine plays, so that the device side keeps building all the way.

## 4. The budget

- **CPU**: 240 MHz, two cores. In fm1-sf2, 32 BFP8 voices with filter cost about 50-60 % at 32 kHz on one core. A D-50 needs 32 partials, each a
  wave generator (or PCM playback), a resonant TVF and a TVA, plus ring modulation, chorus, EQ and reverb. That should fit on two cores, but nothing is
  measured.
- **Flash**: the user region is 536 576 bytes and the wave ROM is 524 288: it fits, with 12 288 bytes left for the firmware's tables. A bank of
  64 patches (28 672 bytes) does not fit beside it and goes in the image or another region (milestone 5).
- **SRAM**: tight. fm1-sf2 does not boot under 111 468 bytes of heap. The reverb's delay lines at 32 kHz have to fit (fm1-sf2's reverb and chorus take 16 KB).
- **The ROMs are Roland's.** Nothing from them goes into this repository or a release. The user gives the install page the dumps of their own
  D-50's wave ROM and EPROM, and it uploads the wave ROM and the tables it needs, as a bank is uploaded today. Without them, the firmware says what is missing.
- **Licences**: the firmware links the FM-1's closed SDK, and the linking exception covers only code that carries it. Our own code and Munt
  (LGPL) may go into a published firmware. Nuked-MT32 (GPL-2.0+) and PicoFaceD5 (GPL-3.0+) may not, unless their authors grant the exception
  (RESEARCH.md section 3).

## 5. Milestones

Each one is played in fm1-emu before it goes to the device, as in fm1-sf2.

1. **Research**: done 2026-11-19, [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md). Still open: reference recordings of a real D-50 if there is access to one.
   - **1b. The PC reference**: MAME's uPD78312 core on the user's IC25 + IC22, Nuked-MT32's LA32; first to find the firmware's LA32 writes, then to
     render reference notes. Runs beside milestones 2-4.
2. **One synth partial**: square/saw with pulse width, TVA envelope, MIDI note on and off, 32 kHz through the existing hook. Measure its cost.
3. **A whole tone**: two partials, the 7 structures with the ring modulator, TVF with resonance, pitch envelope, the LFOs, key follow, velocity.
   A WebAssembly test for each, as fm1-sf2's `test_filter.js` and `test_lfo.js`.
4. **The patch**: Upper and Lower, the key modes, the voice allocation over 32 partials, CPU1 for half of them.
5. **SysEx**: the D-50's DT1/RQ1 address map; a `.syx` bank loads and plays. Patches in the user flash.
6. **PCM partials**: the wave ROM uploaded to the flash, attack and loop playback as the LA chip does it.
7. **Effects**: EQ, chorus, then the reverb types.
8. **The panel**: patch select, the edit pages on the FM-1's screen and encoders, the 27 keys.
9. **Faithfulness**: A/B against the reference recordings, curve by curve.
